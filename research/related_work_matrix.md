## Formal Novelty & Academic Contribution Assessment

### 1. What Has Already Been Studied
- **Tree Ensembles on Tabular Data:** Extensive benchmarks (Grinsztajn et al. 2022; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022) have already demonstrated that gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost, LightGBM, HistGradientBoosting) outperform standard and modern deep neural architectures on typical tabular datasets.
- **Feature Selection & Gating:** Instance-wise feature gating (TabNet by Arık & Pfister 2021; Gated Residual Networks in TFT by Lim et al. 2021) and group sparsity (Scardapane et al. 2017) are established paradigms in deep learning. Grouping features or applying softmax gating is **not by itself novel**.
- **BRFSS 2015 Heart Disease Classification:** Numerous community studies (Teboul 2020) and peer-reviewed articles (Khan et al. 2024) have evaluated binary classifiers on this Kaggle subset, frequently reporting high accuracies (>90%) that simply reflect the majority-class prevalence.

### 2. What Our Study Contributes
Our research provides four distinct, auditable scientific contributions:
1. **The Prevalence Inconsistency Proof:** We formally demonstrate that the published metrics in Khan et al. (2024) (91.7% accuracy, 96.0% precision, 87.8% recall) are mathematically inconsistent with the natural 9.42% positive prevalence of the CDC BRFSS dataset (which would require 98.51% accuracy), documenting that published claims likely stem from artificial test-fold balancing rather than real-world population screening.
2. **Inductive Bias Mismatch Demonstration:** We show both theoretically and empirically that passing tabular columns sequentially through 1D CNN kernels and GRU hidden states (as in EnsCVDD-Net) imposes invalid spatial and temporal ordering assumptions, resulting in degraded ranking performance ($0.3563$ PR-AUC).
3. **Domain-Grouped Neural Architecture Ablation & Honest Neutral Finding:** We evaluate physiologically grounded grouping (*Clinical History*, *Lifestyle*, *Self-Reported Health*, *Access/Demographics*) against 1D sequences and uniform pooling. We report an **honest neutral/negative finding**: while domain grouping outperforms 1D column convolutions ($0.3689$ vs. $0.3563$, paired $\Delta = +0.0126$, $p = 0.00027$), learned softmax gating provides **no ranking advantage** over uniform group pooling ($0.3689$ vs. $0.3693$, paired $\Delta = -0.000411$, $p = 0.293$).
4. **Leakage-Safe Multi-Seed Protocol with Persisted Artifacts:** We establish a rigorous benchmark where preprocessing, PBCC ranking, class weighting, threshold selection, and probability calibrators are strictly isolated to training/validation partitions. We persist 951,300 individual test predictions, verified with 0.0000000000 discrepancy against summary tables.

### 3. What Claims Are Defensible
- **Defensible:** Domain-grouped neural architectures reliably outperform 1D convolutional/recurrent column hybrids across all 5 random seeds on held-out test data.
- **Defensible:** Histogram Gradient Boosting remains the superior model overall on this tabular dataset (PR-AUC 0.3717).
- **Defensible:** Learned softmax gating adds parametric complexity without improving ranking performance compared to equal-weight group averaging.
- **Defensible:** Validation-fitted Isotonic regression and Platt scaling successfully restore probability calibration, dropping test Brier score from $0.1654$ to $0.0703$ (an 18% improvement over the $0.0853$ constant-prevalence baseline).
- **Defensible:** A validation-only recall-constrained threshold policy reliably achieves $\approx 79.8\%$ sensitivity on held-out test sets, operating at $\approx 24.6\%$ precision (3 false positives per true case).

### 4. What Would Be Overclaiming (Strictly Avoided)
- **Overclaiming:** Claiming FGGN or any neural architecture is superior to gradient-boosted trees.
- **Overclaiming:** Claiming that learned softmax gating provides a performance breakthrough or superior ranking.
- **Overclaiming:** Claiming that neural gate weights or tree SHAP attributions represent causal biological factors or medical risk drivers.
- **Overclaiming:** Claiming that calibrated probabilities or thresholded predictions are clinically validated or ready for individual diagnostic deployment.
- **Overclaiming:** Claiming that subgroup performance analysis constitutes proof of algorithmic fairness or the absence of bias.
